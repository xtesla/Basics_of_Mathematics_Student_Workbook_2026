# Exercise 15 - Classifying Three Systems

## Problem Statement

For each of the following systems, perform only as much elimination as necessary to recognize its type:

1. $\begin{cases}x+y=2,\\x-y=0,\end{cases}$
2. $\begin{cases}x+y=2,\\2x+2y=4,\end{cases}$
3. $\begin{cases}x+y=2,\\2x+2y=5.\end{cases}$

For each, justify the classification: one solution, infinitely many solutions, or no solution.

## Theoretical Background and Concepts

Elimination identifies a unique solution, a redundant equation, or a contradiction without always requiring a full solution.

## Step-by-Step Solution

**1.** Subtract the second equation from the first: $2y=2$, giving $y=1$, $x=1$ (one solution).

**2.** Subtract twice the first equation from the second: $0=0$ (infinitely many solutions $x+y=2$).

**3.** The same operation gives $0=1$ (no solution).

## Verification and Consistency Checks

The three outcomes show one independent intersection, two identical lines, and two inconsistent parallel lines.
